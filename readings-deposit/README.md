# Two readings of what the rules require, and what they require you to explain

Two measurements of published instruments, made in September 2026 and deposited
so that their dates are held by somebody other than their author.

## What each file is

| file | what it is |
|---|---|
| `obligation.md` | The first reading. Four instruments read against whether they require a record to name who authorised an action, and to be checkable by somebody other than the party that produced it. |
| `explaining.md` | The second reading. Two articles of the EU AI Act read against each other: what a deployer must be able to explain, and what the same regulation requires them to record. |
| `instrument-readings.json` | The data behind the first reading. Every verdict with the clause, control or criterion it rests on. |

Both documents are also published at
<https://machinetestimony.org/obligation/> and
<https://machinetestimony.org/explaining/>, and are generated from the same
source files as those pages so that the deposit and the site cannot state
different findings.

## What they found

**Of four instruments that will oblige deployers to keep records** — the EU AI
Act, the ForHumanity certification criteria, the CoSAI Risk Map and the NIST AI
RMF — three specify what a record must contain. All three require one to be
kept. None requires it to be capable of being shown unaltered by anyone other
than its author.

**Of the eight areas of Annex III to the EU AI Act**, seven carry a right for an
affected person to be told the main elements of a decision. One has the content
of its log specified. They are not the same one and the wider obligation has no
recording requirement behind it.

## What rests on the census, and is not repeated here

Both readings cite counts over ten deployed agent memory and agent framework
systems: how many can name the person who approved an action, how many produce
records verifiable without the vendor, how many can say where a stored fact came
from, how many can say afterwards that two facts disagreed.

Those per-system verdicts are not in this deposit. They are in the conformance
census, **doi:10.5281/zenodo.22290922**, where each cites a file and a line at a
full forty-character commit. This deposit does not repeat them, because a
measurement should be deposited once and cited thereafter.

## A correction, recorded rather than made quietly

An earlier version of the first reading said the EU AI Act contained no
requirement to record the identity of a person who authorised an action. That
was false. Article 12(3)(d) requires the logs of a remote biometric
identification system to provide "the identification of the natural persons
involved in the verification of the results", referring to Article 14(5), which
requires the action to be separately verified and confirmed by at least two
natural persons. Article 12(3) was read and its scope was not.

The corrected reading is the stronger one: the requirement was drafted, and
confined to a single row of Annex III. Both documents say so on their face.

## What these are not

Not legal advice, and not an assessment of whether any instrument is good at
what it is for. Public text only: ISO/IEC 42001 has no verdict because it is
paywalled, which is recorded as a fact about the field rather than guessed at.

The second reading turns partly on whether Article 86 can be invoked before
December 2027. It states both readings of that question and asserts neither,
and its finding does not depend on the answer.
