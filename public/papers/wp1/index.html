Popularity Is Not Association

A belief-forming rule audited against 19,668 respondents, and what it was measuring instead

Troy Clifford

Machine Testimony Working Paper No. 1  ·  September 2026  ·  Text CC BY 4.0

Abstract

A system that forms beliefs about people from partial evidence was tested against a population whose correlation structure the author did not choose. Two defects were found, and both were load-bearing.

The rule that mined regularities recovered the dataset's known five-factor structure at 0.185, against a chance rate of 0.184. It was measuring how common a claim was, not whether two claims were associated. Separately, the confidence attached to a newly formed belief was a linear score being reported as a probability, and scored substantially worse than a system that ignores its own record and predicts the population base rate every time.

Both were repaired and re-measured. Structure recovery reached 0.875, on 94 per cent fewer regularities that nonetheless covered more claims than the unfiltered rule. Confidence reached parity with the base rate and did not exceed it, which is a removed negative rather than a demonstrated positive, and this paper is explicit about the difference. The repaired system's behaviour at volume is reported, together with a retraction of an earlier finding that the first defect had caused.

Section 1Why a synthetic world could not answer this

The system under test forms provisional beliefs about people. It observes what someone holds to be true, mines regularities across a population, and uses them to form a hunch about a person on a question they have not answered — attaching a confidence to it, and refusing when the evidence is too thin.

Until this study, the only harness for that machinery generated its own population. That arrangement can confirm the mechanism runs, and it can confirm the mechanism recovers structure that was deliberately placed in front of it. It cannot tell you whether the rule finds structure in people, because the structure was chosen by the same person who wrote the rule.

A rule that measures the wrong thing will pass such a test whenever the wrong thing happens to be correlated with the right one in the world that was built. That is what had happened here, and it went unnoticed for as long as the only judge was a world of the author's own construction.

Section 2Data

Open Psychometrics' Big Five item responses, collected around 2012 through an online personality test, with consent recorded at the time and published for research. Fifty items rated one to five. 19,719 responses were collected; 19,668 remained after dropping those who answered fewer than twenty items.

The dataset is downloaded when the benchmark runs and is not redistributed by this programme or its repositories. Nothing here republishes another person's survey response.

Two properties made it the right instrument, and the second is the one that matters.

The mapping to the system's semantics is almost exact. An answer of four or five is holding a proposition, one or two is holding its negation, and three — the neutral answer — is a genuine silence. The system only forms a hunch into a silence, so the experiment gets its held-out question for free: hide an answer somebody actually gave, and ask whether the system can put it back.

The latent structure is known. Fifty items, five factors, ten items each. A pair drawn at random joins two items of the same factor with probability 9/49, which is 0.184. That supplies a ground truth for whether the rule finds real psychology or noise — the thing no self-generated world can supply.

Section 3The rule was measuring popularity

The mining rule required that a regularity clear a support floor and then hold at a reliability rate: of the people who hold P, at least sixty per cent must also hold Q.

Against the known structure, the regularities it produced joined same-factor items at 0.185. Chance is 0.184. The rule recovered none of the structure it was supposed to be finding.

The cause is visible in a single number. The consequents of mined regularities had a mean base rate of 0.76, against an overall item mean of 0.57. If seventy-six per cent of everyone holds Q, then "sixty per cent of the people who hold P also hold Q" is satisfied by Q being common, and says nothing whatever about P. The rule was a popularity detector wearing the interface of an association detector.

The repair, and why a lower bound rather than a rate

A regularity is now required to clear the consequent's own base rate by a margin — and to clear it on the lower bound of its rate, not the rate itself. A raw rate treats three cases out of three as certainty. A Wilson lower bound does not, so a pair resting on a handful of people must be markedly cleaner than one resting on hundreds to earn the same standing.

Within-factor is the share of mined regularities joining two items of the same factor. Claims covered is the number of held-out answers the system was willing to form a hunch about; marginal lift is the improvement over the base rate on those answers.

Recovery reaches 4.8 times chance, marginal lift triples, and the rule does this while forming opinions about more claims than the unfiltered version, not fewer. Ninety-four per cent of the regularities are gone and coverage went up. That is the signature of a filter removing entries that fired constantly and carried nothing.

An in-sample choice, disclosed

The margin was set at 0.10 although 0.15 scored better in the sweep. The sweep was run on the same data it was evaluated against, so the better number is not evidence that it generalises; 0.10 already beats the incumbent on every axis including coverage, and the smaller margin concedes less to a fit that may be an artefact. This remains an in-sample choice and is reported as one.

Section 4Confidence was not a probability

Once the mining rule was repaired, the same harness could be turned on the other half of the machinery: how bold a newly formed belief is at birth.

That number was a base constant plus fixed steps for each supporting verdict, clamped at both ends. It is not the probability of anything, but it was being consumed as one, and the system's own calibration benchmark was scoring it as one.

Brier skill is measured against always predicting the population base rate. Zero is parity with that baseline; negative is worse than it.

What −0.003 is, and what it is not

Parity is not skill. A Brier skill of −0.003 means the repaired estimator is statistically indistinguishable from a system that ignores every record it holds and predicts the population base rate every single time. It is not evidence that the system's confidence carries information about which beliefs will hold.

What was removed is a large negative: an estimator actively worse than knowing nothing, whose output was being read as a probability by everything downstream. Removing that matters. Claiming more than that would be the sort of thing this programme exists to check.

Three findings, in the order they appeared

The anchor was the problem, not the estimator. The base constant was 0.35, a guess about a population nobody had observed. Hunches in this population held 68 per cent of the time. Moving the anchor to the system's own measured rate improved skill roughly five-fold, while changing the estimator's functional form barely moved it at all. The house rate is now learned, and is itself shrunk toward the old constant until there is enough record to believe it.

A measurement can be confounded by a design choice. The first sweep reported that a very small pseudo-count scored best. It scored best because confidence was capped below the rate these hunches actually achieve, so the sweep was rewarding whichever setting reached the cap fastest. An internal guard caught the consequence — at that value, a single success took a generator straight to the ceiling — and the constant was set on the principle instead of the score.

Two safeguards were stacking into a fixed error. Every hunch a young installation makes is borrowed, because a handful of people cannot support a mined regularity. A borrowed hunch was capped at the ceiling times a discount, 0.45, while borrowed hunches were holding 65 per cent of the time. That is not caution; it is a twenty-point error no amount of evidence could correct. Borrowing now raises the evidence bar rather than capping the output: a borrowed regularity needs more of its own record before it moves off the house rate, and once it has proved itself on this installation's people it is not meaningfully borrowed any more.

A rule that is correct and cannot run is not usable. The repaired system was measured against synthetic populations at sizes no real installation has yet reached, on a laptop with 3.8 GB of RAM.

Cost per pair rises with population, because intersecting two bitmasks costs one machine word per sixty-four subjects, which is real work. It does not rise with vocabulary, which is what a cubic loop would do.

Two regressions arrived alongside the repair and were caught by the same measurements. A vocabulary walk had been placed inside the pair loop, making a loop quadratic by design into a cubic one; its signature appeared before its cause, as a per-pair cost climbing with population when it should have been flat. Separately, subject membership was held in ordinary sets, whose intersection is linear in the population; bitmasks measured thirty times faster at a thousand subjects and ninety at fifty thousand.

The pass that only fails when the system is mature

The worse defect was in the pass that forms new beliefs, and it is invisible on a fresh installation because a cap stops the run after twenty-five hypotheses. It appears in the steady state that a mature installation is in on every subsequent pass: most things are already spoken for, almost nothing is eligible, the cap never fires, and the loops run to completion.

The guards written against both regressions pin counts, not seconds — one store scan per pass, at most one vocabulary consultation per proposition — because a timing assertion on a shared runner is a generator of false alarms. Each guard was checked by reintroducing the regression and confirming it failed.

Section 6A retraction

On the day the first defect was found, this programme's own benchmark had reported that pooling regularities across installations fails in a saturated population, where almost everyone already holds the claim in question. The measurement was −0.116 and the number was real. The explanation offered for it was wrong: it was the popularity defect, not a property of pooling.

With the rule repaired, that case runs at +0.264, and the harness's negative control still behaves. The claim is withdrawn here, in the repository's claims ledger, and in the benchmark that made it.

A second, smaller correction belongs in the same section. One of the test suites covering pooled regularities had been passing without testing anything: it recomputed the old formula inline rather than driving the system, so it would have continued to pass after the system's behaviour changed. It has been rewritten to drive the engine.

Section 7What this does not show

  1. The population is not the target population. Personality items are not the working behaviours this system's vocabulary describes. Whether the two resemble each other is untested, and the burden is on anyone claiming they do.
  2. This tests the rule, not the corpus. The shared corpus is empty. No installation has contributed to it. Nothing here establishes that pooling across real deployments works; it establishes that a rule which would have filled that corpus with popularity no longer does.
  3. One dataset, one method, one moment. Self-report, online volunteers, collected around 2012, in one language. Every known limitation of that instrument applies unchanged to every number above.
  4. Recovering a hidden answer is not predicting a person. The held-out design hides an answer somebody already gave. That is a weaker task than anticipating what someone will do, and the numbers should not be read as the latter.
  5. The margin is an in-sample choice. See Section 3. It was selected conservatively for that reason, which reduces the exposure without removing it.

Section 8Reproduction

Everything in this paper is produced by runnable harnesses in the reference implementation. The external dataset is fetched at run time and is not contained in the repository. 

python3 benchmarks/external/run.py      # Sections 3 and 4
python3 benchmarks/scale/run.py         # Section 5
python3 server/tests_prior_lift.py      # the repaired rule, checked
python3 server/tests_scale_mining.py    # the mining guard
python3 server/tests_scale_leap.py      # the belief-forming guard

The full suite stands at 97 passing files across 2,721 checks. The changes described here are recorded in the reference implementation's history, together with the measurements that prompted each one.

Why this was worth publishing

Both defects altered what every installation would learn and what the shared corpus would be filled with. They were found because the system was pointed at a population it did not construct, and they were found before any installation existed to be affected — which is the only reason this paper reports a repair rather than a recall.

A programme that publishes a format for holding automated systems to account has no standing unless it does the same to its own reference implementation, including on the days the measurement comes back badly.
