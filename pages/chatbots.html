Reading, 12 September 2026

Two states require a chatbot to detect a crisis. Neither requires a record that it did.

Companion chatbot statutes are arriving faster than any other kind of AI law in the United States. Three passed in 2026. They share a subject, a date and a structure: the operator of a conversational AI service must disclose that the user is talking to a machine, must detect and respond when a user expresses suicidal ideation or self-harm, and must give minors additional protection.

This page asks them the question the rest of this site asks of everything: when the obligation is not met, what could show it. Two are read here. The third could not be reached and is named rather than guessed at.

Washington asks an operator to keep nothing at all

The enacted text runs to seven pages. Section 4 requires that an operator “may not make available or deploy an AI companion chatbot unless it maintains and implements a protocol for detecting and addressing suicidal ideation or expressions of self-harm by users”. That protocol must include “reasonable methods for identifying expressions of suicidal ideation or self-harm, including eating disorders”, must “provide automated or human-mediated responses that refer users to appropriate crisis resources, including a suicide hotline or crisis text line”, and must implement measures to prevent content encouraging self-harm.

Counted over the whole enacted text: zero occurrences of log, logs or logging as words. Zero of audit. Zero of record. Zero of retain or retention. Zero of report.

The count is worth explaining, because a naive search says seven. All seven apparent matches for log are inside other words: login, technologies, psychologist, methodology. This site made the same mistake once before, on New York City's Local Law 144, where the single occurrence of record turned out to be a penalty schedule about motion picture captioning.

So an operator must implement a protocol that detects a user in crisis and refers them to a hotline. If it does not, nothing in the statute requires the operator to hold anything that would show that.

Colorado requires a description, then reserves a power to check it

Rules 9 to 14 of the same filing this site reads for the ADMT Act implement the Chatbot Safety Act. Counted across them the position is the same: no logging requirement, no audit requirement, and the three occurrences of retain are about a minor's own data, requiring that privacy settings let a minor stop the service retaining prior sessions and default to not retaining them.

What Colorado adds is an annual report. Rule 13 asks an operator to state “Whether and at what point a situation is elevated to human review” and the criteria that decide it. That is a description of a method, not a record of an event.

Then Rule 13.3 reserves a power to check. The Department “may request that an Operator produce underlying documentation, source materials, records, or a demonstration sufficient to verify any element of the submission”, within 30 days. That is the only occurrence of record in the chatbot rules, and it is a demand rather than a duty to keep.

So an operator states annually when a minor's conversation is escalated to a person, and may be required within 30 days to prove any part of that. Nothing requires the proof to exist, and Rule 11.5 pushes the other way by defaulting a minor's data to not being retained. Both rules are defensible. Nothing in the text says which gives way.

Summaries of it exist and are not used here, on the same terms as ISO/IEC 42001 on the instrument reading: a verdict that cites the clause it rests on cannot cite what it has not read. What this reading is not

This is not an argument that either statute should require logs. A safety law about minors has reasons to keep operators away from retaining conversations, and Colorado's own Rule 11.5 is written to do exactly that. An outsider should be slow to second-guess it.

The finding is narrower and it is about a tension rather than an omission. Both statutes create duties whose breach is invisible from anything they require anybody to hold, and one of them separately reserves a power to demand evidence of compliance. Those can both be right. They have not been reconciled in either text, and the operators who will have to live with them on 1 January 2027 have five months to work out what they would produce if asked.

Both readings are of committed text.
